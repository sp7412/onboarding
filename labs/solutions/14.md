# Solution sketch: Lab 14

A promotion policy should name the error definition, target, minimum sample, confidence
level, cost model, and drift/OOD guard before looking at the next result.

## Promotion

Use the Wilson upper bound rather than the observed error rate. Promotion is one level at a
time. A hold is a valid decision.

## Sequential testing

SPRT is useful when waiting for a fixed sample size is unnecessarily expensive. State alpha
and beta up front, then stop when the likelihood ratio crosses a boundary.

## Calibration and drift

Calibration checks whether confidence means what it claims. Drift/OOD checks whether the
traffic still resembles the population on which the policy was validated. Neither replaces
the other.

## Dashboard

At minimum show:
- autonomy level by segment
- error rate and Wilson upper bound
- sample count
- cost-weighted incidents
- false-booking and missed-booking rates
- calibration/ECE
- PSI/OOD
- recent promotion/demotion events
- top failure modes

The important operational property is that a segment can pause or demote without forcing the
entire system to the same autonomy level.
