# Field Exercise: Hear How Contractors Answer the Phone

**Facts as of: October 6, 2026 · Last reviewed: October 6, 2026**

The goal is domain understanding, not competitive intelligence. Keep this exercise ethical,
short, and public-safe.

## 1. Mystery-shop the phone experience

Call 3–5 local HVAC or plumbing companies after hours with a **real, non-urgent question**,
such as asking about tune-up pricing.

Do not:
- invent an emergency;
- pretend to need a booking you do not need;
- cause a technician dispatch;
- record the call without consent;
- collect personal information you do not need.

If asked why you are calling, be honest. Do not name the businesses in this public repo.

Note:
- how quickly the call was answered;
- whether it reached a CSR, answering service, voicemail, or AI;
- what the system asked for first;
- whether it recognized the trade/job;
- how availability was presented;
- whether the caller could interrupt or correct it;
- how escalation worked;
- what the system appeared to treat as authoritative.

## 2. Talk to a contractor

If you know a contractor, ask for 20 minutes. Use the question list in the field-notes
template.

Useful questions:
1. Which calls are most valuable?
2. Which calls are most annoying or expensive to mishandle?
3. What makes a call genuinely bookable?
4. Which mistakes create wasted truck rolls?
5. Which information does dispatch need from the call?
6. What makes a CSR trust or distrust automation?
7. What happens when capacity is full?
8. Which metrics does the owner actually watch?
9. Where do after-hours calls go today?
10. What would make you comfortable letting software act without approval?

## 3. Optional public material

You can also listen to public contractor-facing webinars or podcasts about call booking and
dispatch. Use the existing reading guide and references rather than adding unverified links.
